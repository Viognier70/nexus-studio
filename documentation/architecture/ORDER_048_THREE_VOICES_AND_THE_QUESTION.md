# ORDER 048 — Three Voices and the Question

**Version:** 1.0
**Status:** Awaiting Vision Owner approval. Not in force until approved.
**Class:** Sprint order — production (precedence level 7)
**Parent:** `ORDER_043` v3 + Addenda A/B; `ORDER_047_THE_INSTRUMENTS_OF_SERVICE.md`
**Registry:** register in `ORDER_REGISTRY.md` before execution
**Recipient:** Claude Code

---

## 1. Why this order exists

ORDER 047 shipped and was played on 2026-08-09. The instruments arrived, the stream shortened, speed doubled. The Vision Owner's verdict names eight things, and behind them sits one finding:

**Three different jobs have collapsed into one voice.**

Everything on screen now speaks in the observer's register — long, interpretive, ending in a wondering question. That register was right for one job and has been applied to all of them. The result is that nothing during service reports plainly, and the connection between a choice and its effect is buried in reflection.

This order separates the voices, ties the wager to something the player can see moving, and gives the questions a sender.

---

## 2. The three voices

### 2.1 The service report — plain, short, continuous

What is happening in the dining room and the kitchen, **right now**, in short concrete sentences. Neutral. No interpretation, no "Hm", no question.

> Bord 4 väntar på huvudrätt.
> Två förrätter går ut från passet.
> Diskstationen ligger efter.

**Unaffected by the player's choices** — this is the room reporting itself. It runs throughout service at a readable pace. Short enough that the eye takes it in without leaving the room.

This replaces the ambient stream as the primary service-time channel. Sentence length: a clause, not a paragraph.

### 2.2 The outcome — immediate, attributable

When the player answers a scenario, **the result appears at once**, plainly, and says what followed from the answer. Not a reflection. A consequence.

> Du sa ja. Sällskapet fick fyran och tvåan ihopslagna. Grannbordet flyttades.

It must arrive within seconds of the choice, and it must be visually distinct from the running report — not by colour-coding severity, but by position and timing. This is the fix for the Vision Owner's repeated finding that the connection between answer and effect cannot be seen.

### 2.3 The observer — after service, not during

The four-movement voice from Addendum B is **kept**, and moves to where it belongs: the evening account. Someone describing the night afterwards, with posture and unfinished thoughts.

It is the wrong register for live reporting and the right one for reflection. Nothing during service speaks this way.

**Addendum B is not repealed** — it is relocated. Its acceptance criteria now apply to the evening account alone.

## 3. The meters must show the trade

The Vision Owner's central complaint stands after ORDER 047: **it is still unclear what economic, social and ecological actually mean in practice.**

The fix is not explanation. It is watching one fall while another rises from the same event.

> The delivery of organic vegetables from Bergslagen fails. Ecological falls. The dish stays on the menu because the produce is replaced with conventional — so economic holds, or even rises.

**Every meaningful event writes to at least one sustainability, visibly, at the moment it happens.** Where an event has a genuine trade-off, it writes in opposite directions on two of them, and both movements are visible in the same beat.

Requirements:

- **Real-time movement.** A meter nudges when the event fires, not at service close.
- **Named cause.** The player must be able to tell which line in the report moved which meter. Report how to do this without adding severity styling — position, timing and adjacency are available; colour-coding is not.
- **Trade-offs are the point.** Report which event kinds carry a genuine two-way trade and which are single-direction. If nearly all are single-direction, the sustainabilities will stay abstract however well they are labelled.

This also makes the wager legible for the first time: staking on ecological means something once the player has watched ecological move.

## 4. The question has a sender

Scenarios currently arrive from nowhere. **Each one comes from a named member of the team** — sous-chef, bartender, runner, servitör — in their own words.

> Bartendern: "En gäst vill ha en Old Fashioned. Vet du hur den ska serveras?"

Two consequences:

**The team becomes visible in what is asked.** A hired apprentice asks basic questions. A sommelier does not. The player hears the consequence of their hiring in the questions they are asked to answer — which is truer to running a restaurant than any meter.

**The player's role becomes clear.** They are the one asked, because they are the one who is supposed to know.

Report how sender is chosen: by role fit to the theme, by lowest competence in the relevant axis, or a mix.

---

## 5. Concrete questions, asked where they matter

**This reverses a decision.** Addendum A rejected quiz questions. That rejection was aimed at *quiz as a side panel* — knowledge tested beside the restaurant, scored for points. The Vision Owner's proposal is a different thing and the earlier rejection was too broad.

> A guest orders a drink not on the list. The bartender asks whether you know how an Old Fashioned is made, how it is served, what garnish it takes.

This is **knowledge tested at the moment it is needed, by the person who needs it, about a guest who is waiting.** It is not beside the game; it is the game. It is also the first appearance of the constitution's *seeking knowledge* limb (`DESIGN_BACKLOG.md` B-010) in playable form.

Requirements:

- **Attached to a scenario**, never free-standing. The question exists because a situation produced it.
- **Concrete and answerable** — a real professional question with a real answer, not a judgement call. This is *episteme* and *techne*; the judgement call is the surrounding scenario, which remains *phronesis* without a key.
- **Answering feeds the enablers** per `ORDER_043` §3.3 — competence still derived, still not purchased, still not shown as a score.
- **Getting it wrong has a consequence in the room**, not a red cross. The drink goes out wrong; the guest notices; a meter moves.

Report the question format and how many are needed for one cycle before writing them.

---

## 6. Scenarios must be complex enough to be worth answering

The Vision Owner: *the choices do not demonstrate the ability to handle complex situations.*

Three responses to a single dilemma is thin. Report what would raise it — a second decision that follows from the first, a choice whose right answer depends on the current state of the meters, a situation where two of the sustainabilities genuinely conflict.

**Do not raise the count of scenarios to compensate.** Depth, not volume.

---

## 7. The player must know what to do

No instruction exists anywhere. The player is not told that 3 shows the quarter and its guest flow, or that 4 opens the restaurant.

- **Name the keys** where the player will see them, in plain Swedish, without a tutorial overlay.
- **The countdown must be unmissable** — the player should never wonder whether something is about to happen or whether they are meant to act.

---

## 8. The arrival mark still does not exist

Addendum B §5A.7 specified an animation and a sound on each new line. Neither is perceptible in play. Diagnose whether they fire at all, and make the arrival visible and audible at 2× and 4× speed — which is where it now matters most.

---

## 9. Acceptance

The Vision Owner plays one dinner and reports:

1. That the running report reads as a status of the room — short, concrete, glanceable.
2. That answering a scenario produced an immediate, plainly stated outcome he could attribute to his answer.
3. That he watched one sustainability fall and another rise from the same event, and understood the trade.
4. That each question came from someone, and that a thin team was audible in what he was asked.
5. That at least one concrete professional question was asked at the moment it mattered.
6. That he knew what to press and when.
7. That he noticed each new line arriving.

---

## 10. What this order does not do

No material or finish work. No persistence. No Måltidens Hus — §5 is the seed of knowledge-seeking inside service, not the institution.

---

## 11. What this order is for

ORDER 043 made an evening happen. ORDER 047 made it measurable. This order makes it **legible in the moment** — a room that reports plainly, a choice that answers back, meters that show what a decision cost and what it bought, and questions that come from people who are waiting for you to know.

The observer's voice was right and was asked to do too much. Given one job, it will be better at it.
