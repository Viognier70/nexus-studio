## Why this addendum exists

The full day was played on 2026-08-08: opening image, prep, rhythm-curved service, stream, staff, scenario, close. The Vision Owner's verdict:

> It was fairly eventless, and the descriptions were rather dull without narrative framing, which makes it stiff.

Asked to describe an evening as he would to a colleague who had been there, he wrote:

> "En gäst i ett sällskap av 3 som beställt samma rätt fick ingen garnityr — ett svagt missnöje spred sig bland gästerna när de började diskutera restaurangens förmåga att leverera mat i samma standard. Hm, märkligt att varken kocken som platade i köksluckan eller servitören märkte något — undrar om de vet vad de serverar?"

Compare with what ships today:

> "En förrätt saknar sin garnityr idag."

The shipped line is correct and empty. The Vision Owner's line has four movements, and the difference between them is the difference between a log and an evening.

**This is also the answer to "eventless."** It felt eventless not because too little happens, but because nothing that happens *feels like anything*. Fifteen observations without interpretation are noise. **Four lines with this structure are an evening.**

---

## §5A.4 (revised) — The four movements

An ambient event is not an observation. It has up to four movements, and the good ones have all four:

**1. What happened.** Concrete, specific, with numbers where numbers matter. Not "a starter" but "one guest in a party of three who ordered the same dish."

**2. What it did to the room.** The consequence lives inside the sentence, before anyone names it. Not "a garnish is missing" but that a mild dissatisfaction spread — and spread *because the guests compared with each other*. The mechanism of the consequence is part of the observation.

**3. Who failed to see it.** Naming the station makes the failure traceable to the team: the chef plating at the pass, the waiter carrying it out. Both saw it and neither stopped it. This is what makes the stream a reading of the staffing rather than a list of mishaps.

**4. The observer's judgement, as a question.** "Hm, odd that neither noticed — do they know what they are serving?" Not a verdict. A question that points toward the hire without delivering the conclusion. The player draws it.

### The observer

**There is someone watching.** The stream is the proprietor walking the room — not a system reporting state. The voice has posture: it notices, it wonders, it lets some things go and stops at others. "Hm" belongs in this voice. So does an unfinished thought.

The observer never issues instructions and never grades. They notice and they wonder.

### Length and rarity

**Fewer events, longer.** A four-movement line is three or four times the length of what ships now, and the event rate must fall accordingly — roughly by half. Report the revised rates.

**Not every event needs all four movements.** A quiet evening may carry a line with only the first two. But the ratio matters: if most lines are bare observations, the voice is gone. Aim for the majority carrying at least three.

**The positive category gets the same treatment.** A table that is well looked after should read as observed by the same person with the same posture — not as a compliment issued by the system.

## §5A.5 The wager is locked at placement

The wager currently offers **Ångra** with no time limit. This makes the stake riskless: place it, read the stream, withdraw if it looks wrong. That is not reading the room — it is waiting for the answer.

**The stake is locked the moment it is placed.** No withdrawal, no time window.

**And the panel must explain what it means.** It currently says "Insatsen står" to a player who has never been told what a stake is. Before placing, the player must be able to see: what is being staked, what a correct reading returns, what a wrong reading costs, and that the choice cannot be taken back. In the observer's voice, briefly — not as a rules panel.

---

## §5A.6 Prep must be visible

The mise-en-place phase shipped, and the Vision Owner did not see it. Either it does not render in the panel or the two-minute window passes before it registers.

Diagnose which, and fix it. Prep lines get the same four-movement treatment: what the kitchen did, what it means for tonight, who was or was not paying attention.

---

## §5A.7 Arrival must be marked

A line that appears silently in a side panel is missed. The eye is on the room.

**Each new event needs a moment in time**: a brief animation as the line arrives, and a sound. Quiet, short, not a notification chime — something closer to a room sound than an interface sound.

Per §A.3 this must not become severity signalling. **The same animation and the same sound for every event**, regardless of kind or weight. The mark says *something has happened*; the text says what.

## Acceptance

The Vision Owner plays one dinner and reports:

1. That the lines read as someone describing the evening, not as a system reporting it.
2. That fewer, longer lines feel like more happening rather than less.
3. That prep was visible and told him something about the team before service began.
4. That he noticed each line arriving without looking away from the room.
5. That the wager felt like a commitment, and that he understood what he was committing to.

If the lines still read as stiff, the fix is in the writing and not in the rate. Do not add more events.
